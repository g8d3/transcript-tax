Transcript Tax 2026: what a "trivial" agent turn really costs

Date: 2026-10-17 · Dataset: /tmp/night/bench.csv (65 runs) · Spend: ~$0.08 Status: working paper, not a benchmark press release. Methods and caveats inline.

TL;DR (claims)

  1. Steady-state floor is ~2.5–3.5 s per no-op turn on every harness tested. Median PONG wall (runs 1–10): pi 2.94 s (100 KB ctx) / 2.42 s (2 MB ctx*), pi-rust 3.26 / 3.35 s, zero 3.06 / 3.36 s. The LLM call itself is milliseconds; the tax is harness overhead.
  2. Transcript size does cost, but sub-linearly with a ~1 s floor, and our stated fit was wrong. Prior session series (n=8 pi sessions, coarse ranges): ~1 s overhead at 30–108 KB, 1.5–1.7 s at 170–228 KB, ~5 s at 3.2 MB and 9 MB. The draft's 1.0 s + 0.5 ms/KB is a mis-stated coefficient — its own table implies ~5–6 ms/KB across 100–230 KB and saturation beyond (see §8). A fresh attempt to reproduce the ladder failed: a 452 KB ingest produced a 108 KB session, and two workspaces with the same 108–111 KB session differed by 1.4 s in median wall.
  3. The 2 MB "ladder" in this dataset is invalid as a size experiment. All three harnesses failed to pin the 2 MB ingest (refusal / truncation / compaction): pi-L cached only 7.7 k vs pi-S 41 k; zero-L compacted 103 k → ~9.4 k by pong 1. Read the grinder as a steady-state + cache-behavior study, not a size sweep.
  4. Cache hits dominate wall time more than token counts do. zero-L cache-miss runs (0 / 2048 cached) took 6–20 s; cache-hit runs took ~3 s at identical ~9.4 k input. Wall correlates with cache state, not with input tokens.
  5. Money implication: compact early, budget per turn. At observed floors, 1,000 no-op turns ≈ 1 hour of wall and the provider bill of whatever your context window carries. A stale 40 k-token context re-sent (or re-read) every turn is the line item, not the model reply.

* The "2 MB" label names the ingest attempt; post-ingest cached context shows it did not stick (see §3).

1.6 Prior finding (verified): profiling pi-core — sessiond exonerated

  • Bare-pi fork (no sessiond) reproduces the ~4.2 s turn: sessiond is not the bottleneck.
  • Startup scans ~200 MB of the sessions directory (I/O tax on every invocation).
  • Within-turn profile: 0.03 s CPU + ~2.6 s idle wait pre-dispatch. Candidates: waitForIdle / activeRun gating, retry backoff. TTFT observed 5–437 ms (wide; provider path, not harness).
  • Net: the floor in §1.2 (unaccounted ~1.5–2 s) is consistent with a startup-scan + idle-gate signature, not model latency.

2. Caveats (honest)

  1. Small n, one night, one machine. n=10 pongs per cell; jitter is ±1 s run-to-run (see min–max in §1.1). Nothing here resolves sub-second harness differences. pi-S mean vs median (3.21 vs 2.94) already shows how one slow run moves the average.
  2. Providers differ across harnesses. pi cells used deepseek-v4.1-flash via opencode-go; pirust used deepseek-chat; zero used its medium model path. Cross-harness gaps (e.g. pirust +0.3 s over pi) confound harness overhead with provider routing. Within-harness patterns (cache-miss penalty, unaccounted dominance) are the robust results.
  3. The size ladder is invalidated (§1.4). Do not fit wall-vs-KB on S vs L in bench.csv. The size curve we do endorse is the prior 8-session series (§1.5), itself small-n and single-harness.
  4. Asymmetric instrumentation. pirust contributes wall-only rows; zero contributes wall + provider usage but no llm/unacc split; only pi-TS has the internal span breakdown. Cross-cell "unaccounted" comparisons are therefore approximate.
  5. Ingest costs are single samples (one run-0 per cell) and include a retry on pi-L and zero-L (attempt-2). Treat $0.003 (100 KB) and $0.035–0.065 (2 MB attempt) as anecdotes, not rates.
  6. Cache behavior is provider-managed and non-stationary. The zero-L miss streak (runs 2–5) may reflect provider-side prefix-cache eviction, compaction nondeterminism, or session-state churn — we record the correlation, not a mechanism.

3. Money implication: compact early, budget per turn

What converts this from latency trivia to a cost line:

  • Every turn re-pays context. Opencode's 31 k re-send (§1.7), pi-S's 41 k cached reload (§1.2), zero's 9.4 k steady state (§1.3) — whichever harness, the context window is re-carried each turn. At $x per MTok input, turns × context_kb is the bill, and cache-hit rate is the discount lever (zero-L: miss = 3–6× wall; provider billing typically mirrors this with cache-miss premiums).
  • Floor cost is wall-clock, not tokens. ~3 s/turn × 1,000 turns ≈ 50 min of agent wall even when the reply is one word. Fleet math (10 agents × 100 turns/day) starts at ~8 wall-hours/day of pure overhead before any useful work.
  • Practical rules: 1. Compact/aggressively summarize past ~100 KB. The §1.5 curve bends upward past ~150 KB and the MB regime buys 5 s/turn regardless of harness. 2. Budget per turn, not per task: turn_budget = floor_s (≈3) + expected_provider_s + input_tokens × price + miss_penalty × miss_rate. Measure your own miss rate (our zero-L sample: 5/10 cold) before promising task SLAs. 3. Prefer harnesses that minimize re-send (Go-layer compaction to ~9 k in zero's case) if the fidelity loss is acceptable — verify task success, not just wall, before switching. 4. ~~Fix the startup scan.~~ Retracted — see §7. Startup does not scale with the session store (298 MB and near-empty workspaces start identically), so this is not a cut we can stand behind. The measured levers are context size and re-send minimization; cache discipline is unconfirmed (§6).

5. Correction (05:45Z, added after §1–§4 were written): the LLM-span clock is not the provider roundtrip

Found while turning the report's own metric into a product guardrail. It qualifies claim 1's attribution and §1.2 directly, so it goes in before anything is published.

5.1 What was measured

llmDurationMs (and each sample's durationMs/ttftMs) is computed from Pi's message_start to message_end hooks. That window is not reliably the provider call. Same prompt, same model, same machine, alternating runs:

run measured span ttft implied rate
short reply ("PONG", 3 tok) 2 ms 1 ms 1500 t/s
short reply ("PONG", 2 tok) 541 ms 534 ms 3.7 t/s
short reply ("PONG", 3 tok) 8 ms 7 ms 375 t/s

A network roundtrip cannot be 2 ms and then 541 ms for the identical request. When the provider delivers the reply in a single chunk, message_start fires at (or after) the first chunk, so the window collapses to ~0 and the tokens/window division fabricates a rate. Reproduced independently twice in this run (one agent via the grinder cells, one via a fresh pi -p matrix).

5.2 What it is not

Measured on the same machine, same night:

step cost
node -e 'process.exit(0)' 20 ms
pi --version (full CLI boot, no model) 247 ms
pi --export --session <file> (parse + load a session, no model) 246 ms
pi -p PONG with -ne (no extension discovery) median 2109 ms
pi -p PONG, all-off (-ne -ns -np --no-themes -nc) median 1785 ms
pi -p PONG, base median 2622 ms

So: session parsing is free, CLI boot is ~0.25 s, extension discovery is ~0.5 s, skills/templates/themes/AGENTS ~0.2 s. None of it explains a 2–14 s turn, and none of it is what llmDurationMs is measuring.

5.3 What this changes in this report

  • Claim 1 (steady-state floor 2.4–3.4 s) stands. It is wall-clock, measured outside the harness.
  • §1.2 does not. "The LLM is not the cost; 1.4–2.0 s is unaccounted" is partly the clock: the request-to-first-chunk provider time lands before message_start, so it is counted as "unaccounted" alongside genuine harness overhead. The dataset cannot split those two. Treat §1.2 as "the harness span we can measure is milliseconds, and the rest is provider + harness combined", not as "the provider is free".
  • Any t/s figure derived from these rows for short replies is unreliable (the 1500 t/s row above is in the same class as the "313 tokens in 9 ms" phantom the monitor already filters). Fixed in e095 v19: a window shorter than 50 ms is recorded unrated instead of as a rate.
  • The provider window is measurable, just not from message_start — see §5.4: turn_start → message_start is 1.2–1.6 s and stable, so a fleet audit can stand on that number even though it cannot split it into dispatch vs provider.
  • §1.6's "0.03 s CPU + ~2.6 s idle wait pre-dispatch" is the most consistent evidence available and should be read as provider or dispatch-gate wait, not as CPU work.

5.4 Follow-up — ANSWERED 06:14Z, and it corrects this section's own advice

This section originally said to instrument from request dispatch via Pi's provider_stream_event. That was wrong: the hook fires with the response, exactly like message_start.

Measured externally instead (pi -p --mode json as a subprocess, every NDJSON line timestamped on arrival, 5 reps, /tmp/night/provsplit/FINDINGS.md):

segment median min–max
boot — process start → first event 929 ms 911–984
turn_start → assistant message_start 1451 ms 1193–1605
message_start → first text delta 1 ms 1–359
first text → message_end 2 ms 2–9
total process wall 2453 ms 2121–2901

The provider roundtrip happens entirely before message_start, which is why llmDurationMs reads milliseconds for a network call. The missing time is now located: turn_start → message_start, 1.2–1.6 s, stable across reps (against llmDurationMs values of 1/88/286/364 ms for the same prompt). It cannot be split further from outside — dispatch, gating and provider latency are inside it — so the deliverable is the bound, not a split. Boot is ~0.93 s per invocation, not the 0.25 s pi --version suggests, and it is invisible in the turn rows because turn_start fires after it.

This also decomposes claim 1's floor. The 2.4–3.4 s steady-state medians in §1.1 are process wall (time pi -p ...), and a one-word turn now measures as 929 ms boot + 1451 ms pre-response + 2 ms stream. Boot is the only component in that sum that is removable by configuration rather than by the provider: a loop that keeps a process warm does not pay it per turn. For a 10-loop fleet at 100 turns/day that is 65 wall-hours a year (see roi.py --warm). Everything else in the sum is either the provider's latency (1.45 s, unattackable from here) or the transcript the turn carries.

6. Second correction (06:02Z): claim 4 (cache state predicts wall) is not supported

Found by re-checking every headline number against bench.csv with analyze.py rather than by hand. This one removes a claim we were about to sell on.

6.1 The test

TL;DR 4 and §1.3 say cache state predicts wall time better than token count does. Group the zero-L PONG runs by cache_tok and look at the walls:

cache_tok wall times (s)
0 10.12
2048 3.27, 3.45
9088 3.11
9216 16.55, 2.94, 3.18
9344 20.00, 6.38, 2.83

The same cache value covers both the slowest and the fastest runs. Of the four slow runs (>6 s), only one reported zero cached tokens; the other three reported 9.2-9.3 k cached. Two runs that reported the lowest non-zero cache (2048) were among the fastest. Across every cell in the dataset, cache_tok == 0 produced walls of 3.27 s and 10.12 s.

6.2 What the data does support

The four slow runs are runs 2, 3, 4, 5 — a contiguous streak that then recovers (runs 6-10 back at ~3 s) while cache_tok moves in the opposite direction (2048 → 9344). A transient provider-side or path-level state fits that shape; a per-run cache property does not. n=10 with no cache variation in the pi cells means this dataset cannot test the cache hypothesis at all — it can only fail to confirm it.

6.3 What changes

  • Do not quote TL;DR 4 or §1.3. "Cache hits dominate wall time", "the predictor is cache_tok", and "miss rate 5 in 10" are all unsupported. The honest statement is: four of ten runs in one cell were 2-6x slower with no measured signature we can name.
  • Still stands: claim 1 (the wall floor, measured outside the harness), claim 3 (the 2 MB ladder is invalid), claim 5's arithmetic (context re-sent per turn is the bill), and the correction in §5.
  • Sales consequence: cache discipline is not the lever this data proves. The levers it proves are the per-turn floor and the multi-second tail — and both are visible in the live-fleet sample (/tmp/night/fleet-scan/FINDINGS.md: unaccounted p50 1.6 s, p90 4.6 s, worst turn 85 s, and a per-model floor that does not move with the model).

7. Third correction (06:09Z): the "startup scans ~200 MB of sessions" claim is not reproducible

§1.6 lists it as a verified prior finding and §3 turns it into a recommendation ("fix the startup scan"). Tested directly:

measurement value
pi --version from a workspace whose session dir is 298 MB 244, 247, 247, 248, 246 ms
pi --version from a workspace whose session dir is tiny 242, 243, 241, 253, 241 ms
pi --export --session <file> (parse + load a session) 246 ms
sessions dir total on this machine 407 MB (the "~200 MB" figure is also stale)

Identical startup cost whether the session store is 298 MB or near-empty, and loading a session file explicitly costs 246 ms — the same as bare startup. Whatever startup does, it does not scale with the session directory. A no-op turn A/B between the two workspaces (3 runs each, --offline -c) also showed no consistent difference (medians 3.1 s vs 2.9 s, inside the ~1 s jitter this dataset already documents).

What changes: §3's rule 4 ("fix the startup scan and the floor drops for every turn") loses its basis and must not be sold. The floor is real and measured; the explanation "startup scan" is not. §1.6's other half — "0.03 s CPU + ~2.6 s idle wait pre-dispatch" — is consistent with everything else measured here (the wait is provider/dispatch, not CPU) and stands.

Cut-list consequence: the levers this dataset supports are context size (§1.5 series) and re-send minimization (§1.7). Cache discipline is unconfirmed (§6). Startup scoping was our unverified guess and should not be sold as a fix.

8. Fourth correction (06:10Z): the size coefficient was mis-stated 10x, and the ladder would not rebuild

Claim 2 is the last surviving lever, so it got the same treatment as the others.

8.1 The stated fit contradicts its own table

segment implied slope
108 → 228 KB 5.8 ms/KB
228 KB → 3.2 MB 1.1 ms/KB
3.2 → 9 MB 0.0 ms/KB

The draft says overhead ≈ 1.0 s + 0.5 ms/KB. At 228 KB that predicts 1.11 s; the table next to it says 1.7 s. The coefficient is off by ~10x. This is a transcription error, not a measurement: the table was right, the fit was wrong.

8.2 Fresh reproduction attempt (n=5 per cell)

Same machine, same model, three workspaces, ingest then five no-op PONGs with -c:

workspace filler ingested resulting session median wall reps
small 24 KB 29 KB 1924 ms 1986, 1924, 1933, 1924, 1893
medium 172 KB 111 KB 4195 ms 2395, 4466, 4195, 3172, 4728
large 452 KB 108 KB 2832 ms 2356, 25726, 2832, 3380, 2495

The ladder would not rebuild: the 452 KB ingest produced a 108 KB session — the same size as the 172 KB one — so the two larger cells are the same transcript size and differ by 1.4 s in median (4.2 s vs 2.8 s). The one real comparison available (29 KB vs ~110 KB) is inside that noise, with a 25.7 s outlier in one cell.

8.3 What this leaves

  • Do not quote the size coefficient. The ranges in §1.5 are the evidence; the fit was never sound and was mis-transcribed on top of that.
  • Compaction is still the most plausible lever (a transcript in the per-turn path can only cost time), but this dataset does not demonstrate it, and our fresh attempt to build the ladder failed for the same reason the grinder's did (§1.4): big ingests do not stick.
  • What a buyer should hear: we measure your curve, per loop, with the transcript sizes your loops actually reach. That is a measurement, and it is what the audit sells — not a coefficient from n=8.
