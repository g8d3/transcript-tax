The finding (65 runs, 3 harnesses, one night)

  • A no-op turn — reply with exactly one word, call no tools — costs 2.4 to 3.4 s median on every harness tested.
  • We measured where the time is, from outside the harness. A one-word turn: 929 ms of process boot, then 1451 ms before the provider's response arrives (1193-1605 ms across reps), then 2 ms of model output. The roundtrip lands before the clock the harness itself reports, which is why tools that read the harness log tell you the model took 2 ms. We cannot split that 1451 ms into dispatch vs provider — nobody outside can — and we say so rather than guess.
  • Transcript size costs something, but we cannot give you a coefficient: our prior series (n=8) shows ~1 s at 30-108 KB, 1.5-1.7 s at 170-228 KB and ~5 s at megabyte scale, and when we tried to rebuild that ladder to fit a slope, the big ingests did not stick (paper section 8). We measure your curve, per loop, at the sizes your loops actually reach.
  • Cache state did NOT predict wall time in our sample — we checked, and we say so. We expected the same 9.4k input to be fast on a cache hit and slow on a miss. Grouping the runs by cached tokens does not separate them: 9216 cached appears in a 16.6 s run and in two ~3 s runs. Four of ten runs in that cell were 2-6x slower with no measured signature, in a contiguous streak that then recovered. We report that as unexplained, not as a cache finding.
  • Rates from short replies are easy to fake and we do not use them. A one-word reply is often delivered in a single chunk, which collapses any window you divide tokens by; we guard that in our own tool rather than quote the number.
  • Fleet math: 1,000 no-op turns burn ~50 minutes of pure overhead. 10 loops x 100 turns a day = ~52 minutes a day of it, before any useful work happens — and ~2.4 wall-hours a day on real work (measured mean 8.7 s/turn).
  • On real work it holds up, and you cannot buy your way out of it. Our own fleet's turn logs (500 live turns, one machine): 8.7 s wall per turn — 34% model, 29% tools, 37% unaccounted (the log rotates, so the window behind those percentages is gone — the exporter and a current window are published in the repo: fleet/, and the monitor's own turn rows show what happens to that 37% once the provider roundtrip is measured: paper §9). Per model, the unaccounted floor barely moves (p50 1.5-2.5 s) while the model slice's p50 ranges from 1 ms to 9.1 s: switching models changes the model slice, not the tax.
  • The tail is worse than the median. p90 unaccounted is 3.4-5.9 s and the worst turns hit 85 s (and 128 s on a provider that was returning errors). At fleet scale you meet that tail often.
  • 4.4% of our turns produced nothing (failed, aborted, empty) in the same sample. We do not quote a dollar figure for that waste: every one of those errors ran on a model with no public list price, so the honest number is the rate, not a cost.

Source: working paper Transcript Tax 2026 (65 runs, spend ~8 cents, methods and caveats inline). We show you the raw CSV, the per-run logs, and the breakdown before we sell you anything.

  4. Lock it in — per-turn budget formula and a regression check you keep running after we leave.

"Isn't this just a benchmark paper?"

The paper is the evidence, not the product. The product is a measurement on your loops plus a regression gate you keep. We deliberately did not publish a cross-vendor ranking: our own caveats (n=10 pongs per cell, provider routing differs per harness, one machine) do not support one.

"Your own report says the LLM is free. That sounds wrong."

It was partly an artifact and we corrected it before publishing (report §5): the LLM span is measured from the harness's own start event, which can fire at the first streamed chunk, so provider time before that lands in "unaccounted". What we stand behind: the measured wall floor per turn, the cache-miss wall penalty, and the token re-spend of context. What we refuse to claim: a clean provider/harness split. That split is Tier 2 instrumentation work, which is exactly what makes it worth paying for.

"We already compact aggressively"

Then prove it in the measurement. In our own dataset a harness compacted 103k tokens down to 9.4k and then grew back; another ingest that was supposed to pin 2 MB cached only 7.7k — less than the 100 KB case. Compaction policies that look right in config often do not stick per turn. We measure cached tokens per turn rather than trusting the setting.

"Why not just switch to a cheaper model?"

That is a quality trade, and it is yours to make. It also does not remove the wall floor, and it can make cache economics worse if the new provider's prefix caching behaves differently. We measure before recommending either.
